A working login screen does not prove your app has working access controls.

For anyone building with AI, two similar-sounding terms deserve separate attention:

Authentication verifies identity: who is this user?
Authorization checks permission: what may this user access or do?

Consider a simple online store. A customer can view their own private order. The store owner can manage orders for that store. Both may sign in, but their access should differ.

The useful product question is not just “Can this person log in?” It is also “Which records and actions should this person have access to?”

Before approving a feature, write down three things:
1. The user or role involved.
2. The information they should access.
3. The actions they should be allowed to take.

This makes the intended behaviour clearer for your developer, your AI coding assistant and your reviewers. It is a planning step, not proof that the implementation is secure.
